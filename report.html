Could an IRW-style warehouse work for ecological detection data?

Yes. The hierarchical models fit with NIMBLE (occupancy, N-mixture, capture–recapture, community models) share the IRW's structure: a unit with a latent state, observed imperfectly through repeated probes. A draft standard keeps id, item, resp, round-trips six textbook datasets with identical model estimates, and the public data it would hold is plentiful and mostly CC0. Five design decisions are open and are yours to make.

Why the two fit

In an item response model a person has a latent trait and answers items with item-specific parameters. In an occupancy model a site has a latent state (occupied or not) and is visited repeatedly with a detection probability. A community occupancy model, with species-level random effects on detection, is a random-item IRT model with species as items. The table below is the mapping that carries the whole proposal.

The draft standard

One CSV in long format, one row per survey observation of one unit for one taxon. It validates under IRW v1.0 (C1–C7) unchanged.

Site 3 has no row for 1999, visit 1: it was not surveyed. That is the rule that does the most work.

The explicit-zero rule

A row exists if and only if a survey was conducted for that (id, item, period, occasion). A resp of 0 means surveyed and not detected. A missing row means not surveyed. No row ever carries an empty resp.

Unbalanced designs are encoded by row count, not NA padding. Multi-species tables carry a 0 row for every species on the list at every survey, so the species list is unique(item). Treating an unvisited site-occasion as a non-detection biases detection probability downward; it is the most consequential encoding error in this domain.

Side tables, borrowed not invented

  • items: scientificName, taxonRank, taxonID, vernacularName (Darwin Core), plus traits. The analogue of IRW item text.
  • units: decimalLatitude, decimalLongitude, coordinateUncertaintyInMeters (Darwin Core, WGS84); holds units in the sampling frame that were never surveyed.
  • dataset: title, creator, licence, citation (EML); samplingProtocol, samplingEffort (Darwin Core); responseType, unitKind, itemKind, closure, multinomialType, distanceBreaks (new).
  • periods: covariates constant across all units within a period.

What does not survive, and why that is acceptable

  • Covariate values at unsurveyed cells. widewt carries 4 and mallard 22 observation-covariate values where y is NA. unmarked stores them; a file whose rows are surveys cannot. They enter no likelihood.
  • Covariates of never-surveyed units. mallard has 4 sites and MHB2014 1 site with no surveys. Their site covariates leave the core file. The sampling frame is what is lost, hence the units side table.
  • Period-level covariates at unsurveyed site × period. crossbill's year is absent at 48 of 2,403 site-years, and colext will not fit with NA there. A covariate that is a function of period alone belongs in the periods table.
  • Multinomial cell structure. alfl's removal design is not recoverable from the table; dataset.multinomialType is required.

What felt forced: the community table is 95% zeros and 7.6 MB from a 0.3 MB array; CJS tables have period but no occasion; the level of a covariate is a property of the design as deposited, not of the variable.

Where the data lives

Ten query terms against each repository's public search API; counts are word-match upper bounds and overlap across sources. The dominant deposited shape is a wide site × occasion matrix with NA for unsurveyed occasions plus a separate site-covariate file: exactly the shape the standard targets. The hard part is not licensing but recovering the survey design from deposits that ship only the analysed matrix.

Volume

  • Seed corpus (weeks): 50–100 tables from R packages, ScienceBase, EDI and the cleanest Dryad deposits.
  • Harvested corpus (months): on the order of 1,000 tables with a Dryad→Zenodo harvester and a journal-supplement sweep. Most have 20–300 units and 2–12 occasions.
  • Big integrations (each a project): BBS, NEON, GBIF sampling-event, eBird. A handful of tables with 105–107 rows that would dominate by volume.

Pitfalls seen in the sample

  • Deposits that pad unsurveyed occasions with 0 instead of NA; only the README tells.
  • Counts pooled over k days with an effort column: the occasion structure is gone.
  • Design encoded in column names (point1.fall2, det991); species as rows.
  • Model outputs mislabelled as data: 30–50% false positives on "occupancy".
  • Raw lat/long for threatened taxa (tapir, large carnivores) in small deposits.
  • xlsx workbooks with one sheet per species; rar archives; MARK .inp.

Licensing is far friendlier than psychology: no ND and no "contact author" deposits in the sample. IRW's intake rules can be adopted unchanged.

Five decisions that are yours

  1. What is item? The draft puts the species on item, so single-species tables look like IRW's repeated-trial family (one item, many occasions) and community tables are species-as-items. The alternative, visit on item, makes every table a person × item matrix an IRT model can fit directly but does not generalise to multi-species data. It is a one-line pivot either way; the choice is about what the canonical corpus looks like.
  2. Dense zeros or a surveys table? The explicit-zero rule makes community tables large (MHB2014: 118,658 rows, 95% zeros) and repeats observation covariates across species. The alternative is Darwin Core's event-core model: a sparse detections table plus a surveys table. The draft recommends dense for IRW-likeness and self-containment.
  3. Sample floor. IRW's 100 ids would exclude most of ecology. The draft proposes ≥ 30 units and ≥ 2 occasions (≥ 2 periods for capture–recapture).
  4. Sensitive-species coordinates. Coarsen by rule (0.1° / 10 km grid, coordinateUncertaintyInMeters set, never finer than the source) versus drop coordinates for any flagged taxon.
  5. How far to stretch. Double-observer and binned distance sampling use occasion for a category; SCR puts traps on item. Accept with a declared itemKind/responseType, or keep v1 to the families where the format is unforced.

What could be done next

  1. A Darwin Core event-core converter, because that is the shape of the largest public sources (GBIF, Swedish Bird Survey, NEON).
  2. A Dryad→Zenodo harvester adapted from the IRW discovery scripts, with a per-deposit "is 0 a non-detection?" gate that refuses deposits where it cannot tell.
  3. One BBS table as the first large integration: id = route, item = species, period = year, occasion = stop, rater = observer.
  4. A validator distributed like irw-validate, with the explicit-zero and covariate-level checks the R prototype already runs.
