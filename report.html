An IRW-style warehouse for ecological detection data

The Item Response Warehouse's long format carries occupancy, count, capture-history and community data unchanged, under local names: unit is the site or animal with a latent state, taxon is the species probed for, y is what the survey recorded, and period and occasion index the repeated surveys. This page codifies the draft standard, points at the repository and the Redivis dataset, lists the deposits collaborators can take next, and shows the first six tables doing something.

The standard, codified

One CSV in long format, one row per survey observation of one unit for one taxon. Column order: unit, taxon, y, y_raw, trap, period, occasion, date, observer, treat, cluster_id, unit_*, period_*, obs_*, taxon_*. The names are local as of v0.3; the IRW's id, item, resp were kept through v0.2 to show the format validates under IRW v1.0, and that is now the job of a one-line rename view, eco_to_irw(), which the validator and the uploader run.

Rule 1 · The explicit-zero rule

A row exists if and only if a survey was conducted for that (unit, taxon, trap, period, occasion). A y of 0 means surveyed and not detected; a missing row means not surveyed; no row ever carries an empty y. Multi-species tables carry a 0 row for every species on the list at every survey, so the species list is unique(taxon). The converter back to a matrix pads with NA.

Rule 2 · Covariates sit at the level where they are constant

The prefix says which sub-model a covariate belongs to without reading documentation: unit_ state, obs_ observation, period_ dynamics. A value that is a function of period alone (the calendar year, the season) is not a column at all; it lives in __periods. A model output estimated from the same detections is not a covariate and is dropped.

Rule 3 · Side tables, named by structure

  • <t>__taxa: key taxon; scientificName, taxonRank, taxonID (GBIF URL), vernacularName, gbifMatchType, traits as taxon_*.
  • <t>__periods: key period; calendar bounds, season, session notes.
  • <t>__units (optional): key unit; coordinates and every unit in the sampling frame, surveyed or not. <t>__traps (SCR): key trap; coordinates and usage.
  • <t>__dataset.json: EML and Darwin Core fields (title, creator, citation, intellectualRights as SPDX, samplingProtocol, coverage) plus responseType, unitKind, closure, stateCodes, multinomialType, coordinatesCoarsened, counts and the floor status. It becomes the Redivis table description.

Rule 4 · Intake

  • Names: author_year_placeOrTaxon_type, lower case, ≤ 40 characters; types occ, count, cjs, dynocc, comm, removal, dobs, ds, scr, state.
  • One table per survey design × response type.
  • Licences as the IRW: CC0, BY, BY-SA, NC and GPL hosted with the licence propagated; ND and unlicensed not hosted.
  • Coordinates never finer than the source; taxa flagged sensitive are coarsened to a 0.1° cell centre with cov_coordinateUncertaintyInMeters set.
  • Proposed floor: ≥ 30 units and ≥ 2 occasions or periods. Tables below it are built but held (open question 3).

A first look at the data

Nothing here is a finding about ecology. The point is that once the tables share a format, the same few lines of code run across all of them, and the species behave like items: each has a prevalence (how many units hold it) and a detectability (how often a survey finds it where it is).

Species as items

Los Lagos carnivores (4)Pampas birds (43)New River fish (74)

Each dot is one species in one community table. Naive occupancy is the share of units with at least one detection; detection rate is the share of surveys that detected it. Fish species are found in 8% of surveys on average but a third of watersheds; the Pampas birds are rarer per survey still.

What a detection model adds

Los Lagos carnivoresPampas birds, first seasonBaird's tapir

A constant-probability occupancy model (unmarked::occu, ψ ~ 1, p ~ 1) fit to each species. Estimated occupancy exceeds naive occupancy for all 24 species, by 0.11 at the median. Dot size is per-survey detection probability p. The Pampas estimates sit at 1.0 because ten sites and p near 0.1 cannot pin ψ down; that is the sample-floor question in one picture.

Effort is a covariate, not a nuisance

New River fish: species detected in a watershed-year against the number of collections that year (obs_effort). The median climbs from 7 species at one collection to about 17 at four or more. A HUC-year with one collection is a survey with low p, not a depauperate watershed.

A capture history read as a longitudinal item

first capturerecapture

Plain-crested Elaenia, nine sessions. The period column is the CJS time axis; recaptures accumulate as marked birds survive, and four histories end early with obs_removed = 1. The same table feeds marked::crm or NIMBLE's dCJS after one pivot.

Open questions

  1. Is the species the right probe? taxon is the required probe column in the draft. Single-species tables then look like IRW's repeated-trial family (one item, many occasions) and community tables are species-as-items, which is where the psychometric payoff is. Visit-as-probe is a one-line pivot and is documented as a derived view.
  2. Dense zeros or a surveys table? The explicit-zero rule multiplies rows by the species count (MHB2014: 118,658 rows, 95% zeros; New River: 74,592 rows, 85% zeros). The alternative is Darwin Core's event core plus a sparse detections table. Dense is self-contained and IRW-like; the draft keeps it.
  3. Sample floor. Two of six built tables fall under the proposed 30 units. The Pampas table has 10 points but 43 species × 20 surveys each, and the figure above shows what 10 sites do to ψ. A floor on units alone measures the wrong axis for community data; a floor on surveys × species, or on identifiability, may be better.
  4. Sensitive-species coordinates. The tapir table coarsened exact camera coordinates to a 0.1° cell; the loon table kept coordinates as the USGS published them. Coarsen by rule (draft) or drop coordinates for any flagged taxon?
  5. How far to stretch. Double-observer and binned distance sampling use occasion for a category. SCR is settled by the rename: with taxon fixed, traps get their own trap key column. Accept the category reading of occasion with a declared responseType, or keep v1 to the unforced families?
  6. Where side tables live. Provisionally settled on 2026-10-31: one Redivis dataset holds core and side tables together, and a table's kind is defined by its structure (its key column and suffix), not by which dataset it sits in. The IRW's separate item-text datasets exist because of a 1000-table cap that eco is nowhere near. Recorded as section 12 of the draft; reversible.
  7. Effort and the survey design. Deposits rarely ship an explicit list of conducted surveys. The rule so far is to infer it from the NA pattern and cross-check against an effort table where one exists (it did for the fish and the tapir). Should a __surveys side table be required whenever the inference is not checkable?

These were inspected in the discovery pass, have recoverable non-detections, and nobody has claimed them. How a table gets built

  1. Check queue/candidates.csv and the dictionary, then claim the deposit.
  2. Write data/<table>.R: header comment with source, licence, paper, deposited layout, mapping and what is not shipped; assertions for every fact the README states; array_to_eco() or a hand pivot; make_taxa() for the taxa; write_table(), which validates and writes the side files; stage_dict_row() and stage_tag_row().
  3. Run Rscript validate/validate_all.R: the draft validator on every table and irw-validate on its IRW view.
  4. Ship with python3 upload/red_up_eco.py, which wraps IRW's red_up: true replace, count(*) against the draft, descriptions from __dataset.json. Nothing publishes; releasing the Redivis version is a human click.
